Pneumonia is not confirmed or excluded by a single test. MedlinePlus describes a diagnosis built from the whole picture: "Sometimes pneumonia can be hard to diagnose", partly because "it can cause some of the same symptoms as a cold or the flu". The provider "will take your medical history", "will do a physical exam", and "may order various tests, such as: A chest x-ray" — one listed test among several, alongside blood tests and a blood culture.

NHS describes the X-ray the same way: "You may be sent for an X-ray of your chest and blood tests to check for other conditions."

Symptoms also vary, so a person can be ill without the textbook picture: "The symptoms can vary for different groups"; "Newborns and infants may not show any signs of the infection"; "Older adults ... may have fewer and milder symptoms" and "sometimes may feel weak or suddenly get confused."

Severity, not the image alone, decides where care happens: "You may need to be treated in a hospital if your symptoms are severe or if you are at risk for complications."

Note for this project: a model output of "Normal" is a research signal, not a diagnosis. Only a clinician who has the history, the examination and the test results can rule pneumonia in or out.
